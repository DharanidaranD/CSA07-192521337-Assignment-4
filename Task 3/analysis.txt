 Link to main building 420 m: FAILS for any copper. Use fibre. Multimode OM3/OM4: 1000BASE-SX 550 m OK, but
                            10GBASE-SR only 300 m (OM3) / 400 m (OM4) < 420 m -> choose single-mode OS2 (10GBASE-LR 10 km).
 T568A at one end + T568B at other = a CROSSOVER cable (pairs 2 and 3 swapped: orange <-> green).
 Modern switches/NICs with Auto-MDIX (and 1000BASE-T, which uses all 4 pairs and negotiates pair swap) work fine.
 Problems remain with old hubs/switches without Auto-MDIX, mislabelled "straight" cables, hard-to-trace faults,
 and failing channel certification. Re-terminate to one standard (T568B) end to end.

ALTERNATIVE - 420 m: MULTIMODE FIBRE vs Cat6A + switch half-way
 Cat6A limit 100 m per segment -> 420 m needs ceil(420/90) = 5 segments = 4 intermediate switches (a single half-way
 switch gives 2 x 210 m, still too long). Each needs mains power, weatherproof cabinets, UPS; extra failure points; ~0.1-0.5 ms/hop.
 Multimode: no intermediate equipment, 2 transceivers (~1 W each), passive cable. Limits: 1G-SX 550 m ok;
 10G needs OM4 <= 400 m (too short) -> single-mode for 10G.
 Lightning: copper between buildings carries induced surge and ground-potential differences into switch ports (needs
 surge protectors/earthing at every hop). Fibre is a dielectric, immune to EMI and surge; only metallic armour/strength members need earthing.
 Decision: single-mode (or OM4 for 1G) fibre.
